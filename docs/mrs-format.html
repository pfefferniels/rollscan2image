MRS Scanner Raster Format

The .mrs and .mrsc files a Welte roll scanner leaves behind are two rasters of the same roll in one shared container. This is what is in them, and how the colour one turns into the PNG delivered alongside it.

01

The container

Both files open with a 124-byte ASCII header of three fixed-width fields, then run pixel data broken into chunks. Every chunk is preceded by a ten-byte counter, $%09d, starting at $000000000 and incrementing; the sentinel $999999999 closes the data.

Datasize counts the markers as well as the pixels, which is why it does not divide evenly by the line length. For the colour file: 44,507 × 6,294 pixel bytes plus 44,508 × 10 marker bytes gives exactly the 280,572,138 the header declares.

02

Two rasters, one roll

The scanner records the roll twice in the same session: a backlit monochrome raster used for note detection, and a reflective colour raster used for the picture. They share the along-roll step and the across-roll orientation, but not the across-roll resolution, and neither is derived from the other.

The across-roll figures come from the paper edges measured against Roll Width = 328.5 in the trailer; a second estimate from the track pitch (100 tracks between 6.5 mm and 322 mm) agrees within about one percent. Pixels are not square in either file, and noticeably less so in the colour one.

Matching note patterns across the two gives mrsc_line ≈ 1.0005 × mrs_line − 570: the same along-roll step to within 0.05 %, with the colour scan starting some 570 lines (about 11 cm) further into the roll and running about 1,000 lines past the end of the monochrome one.

The 1,802-byte trailer on the .mrs is plain INI: a [Soundtrack 1] block (Träumerei Op 15, Schumann, played by Alfred Grünfeld), a [Rolltype] block describing Welte Rot with its 100 tracks, and a [Definition] block with the catalogue number and paper type.

On lateral drift: the paper edges hold to within ±2 px over all 44,000 lines in both files. Whatever else these rasters need, straightening does not appear to be it.
03

From .mrsc to the delivered PNG

The PNG is 44,489 × 2,098 — eighteen columns short of the colour raster’s 44,507 lines, which is the first clue. Three steps, all of them parameterised by the settings CSV written beside the scan, account for the difference. Nothing is resampled: the PNG carries the .mrsc at its native resolution, one scan line per column and all 2,098 samples per column. The monochrome raster is a different matter, about 15 % finer across the roll and unrelated to the PNG’s grid.

STEP 1 Register the colour rows The sensor is trilinear, its rows MRSC_SpatialCorrOfOneColor = 9 lines apart. Green is taken 9 lines later than blue, red 18. Those 18 lines are the ones lost.
STEP 2 Divide out the flat field A parabola across the sensor, its three coefficients in the CSV, is normalised to its vertex and divided out — up to 2.4× at the sensor ends, 1.0× at the centre.
STEP 3 Mirror across the roll Sample s becomes row 2097 − s. The colour raster itself is not mirrored relative to the monochrome one; only the PNG is.
png[x, 2097 − s, c] = floor( mrsc[x + lag[c], s, c] × Cmax / curve(s) ) lag = (R 18, G 9, B 0) from MRSC_SpatialCorrOfOneColor = 9 curve(s) = A·s² + B·s + C A = −7.469772303e−05 Cmax = curve at its vertex B = 0.15701233763037 ≈ 141.59 at s ≈ 1051 C = 59.0841072672962

Reconstructed this way, 93–94 % of pixels come out bit-identical to the scanner’s own PNG and no pixel differs by more than one level in 255, checked at forty places along the roll. The remainder is presumably a rounding detail in the scanner’s implementation; floor matches better than round, but not perfectly.

04

Converting

mrs2image.py reads either file and writes uncompressed TIFF or PNG. The TIFF is single-strip, contiguous, photometric 2, with inch resolution tags, which is the shape roll-image-parser’s TiffFile reader accepts. Default output runs the roll down the image, columns across it, as tiff2holes expects; that reader rejects photometric 1, so a monochrome scan needs --rgb.

# what is in a file python3 mrs2image.py WR0225_02_….mrsc --info # the scanner's own picture, as TIFF python3 mrs2image.py WR0225_02_….mrsc roll.tif --like-png # raw pixels, no processing, a 500-line excerpt python3 mrs2image.py WR0225_02_….mrsc probe.png --lines 20000:20500 # the monochrome scan, expanded to RGB for tiff2holes python3 mrs2image.py WR0225_02_….mrs mono.tif --rgb

--like-png bundles the three steps above; --register, --flat-field and --mirror apply them one at a time. Without any of them the output is the raw raster, which is dark and heavily vignetted but untouched.

05

Still open

  • Q1 Whether MRS Co Nr and MRS SW Nr really name the colour and monochrome modules. The reading fits which file carries which, but nothing in the data confirms it.
  • Q2 The last 6 % of PNG pixels, each off by one level. Likely a rounding or precision difference in the scanner’s own arithmetic, but the exact rule has not been recovered.
  • Q3 Why the two sensors differ in across-roll resolution at all, and why neither has square pixels.
  • Q4 The unit of Spurdistanz = 1.18686868686869 in the .dsp file. It is exactly 117.5/99, which does not match the 3.187 mm track pitch the trailer implies.
  • Q5 Whether the chunk size is fixed per module or just a write-buffer size that could differ on other scans. The converter measures it rather than assuming.
